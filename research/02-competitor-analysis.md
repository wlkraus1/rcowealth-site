# Competitor Analysis — Premium / Virtual Wealth Management & Fee-Only RIA Websites

**Prepared for:** Rae & Co Capital (d/b/a RcoWealth) — SC-registered RIA, virtual, Greenville-area
**Date:** 2026-06-06
**Brand context:** Navy `#06111f` + gold `#c8a15a`, elegant serif + clean sans, tagline "Premium advice. No office visit required."
**Method:** Direct review of competitor homepages plus secondary sources (cited at end). Scores are the analyst's opinionated 1–10 read, not vendor-supplied metrics.

## 1. Competitor Breakdowns

### 1. Facet — facet.com
- **Positioning / headline:** "Financial planning for the life you want."
- **Sub / value prop:** "Feel confident you're making the right money decisions. With Facet's flat-fee membership, you'll get objective advice you can trust." Three pillars: **Objective / Personalized / Comprehensive.**
- **Hero approach:** Single bold serif-ish headline, warm lifestyle photography (families, parents), generous whitespace, one primary CTA.
- **Color palette:** White base, blue/teal diamond accent mark, dark text. Bright, optimistic, "approachable fintech."
- **Typography:** Clean modern sans for body; large display headline. Friendly, not luxury.
- **Conversion mechanics:** Primary CTA **"Get started"** (repeated). Flat-fee membership pricing shown openly ($2,350+/yr Core). Case-study cards by life scenario (new parents, college, early retirement) act as self-qualifiers.
- **Trust signals:** **24,000+ members**, SEC-registered, **CFP® professionals**, **#1 Best Financial Advisory Firm 2025 (USA TODAY/Statista, 30,000+ surveyed)**, press logos (NYT, WSJ, Forbes, Business Insider). Endorsements from Ramit Sethi and Tyler Gardner.
- **What makes it feel premium / strong:** Fee transparency as a trust weapon, named expert endorsements, ranking badge, commission-free/fiduciary framing front and center. *Reads more "accessible-premium" than luxury.*

### 2. Domain Money — domainmoney.com
- **Positioning / headline:** "Turn your financial *dreams* into *reality*" (italic emphasis on emotion words).
- **Sub / value prop:** "Get ahead with flat fee, expert financial advice for investing, taxes, real estate and more."
- **Hero approach:** Emotional headline + quantified benefit stack immediately below.
- **Color palette:** Modern blue/teal + heavy whitespace.
- **Typography:** Contemporary sans; italics used to humanize the headline.
- **Conversion mechanics:** CTA **"Free Strategy Session"** (high-intent, consultative). Flat-fee project tiers ($3,200–$7,800). Dedicated CFP® "no rotating advisors" promise.
- **Trust signals:** All advisors **CFP®**, fiduciary duty stated, **Trustpilot 5-star**, money-back guarantee badge, "zero AUM fees" badge, research citations (Vanguard, T. Rowe Price).
- **Standout:** **Quantified value props** — "3% higher returns," "2–4x more wealth by retirement," "350+ hours saved/yr." This is the single best lesson for Rae & Co: turn benefits into numbers.

### 3. Zoe Financial — zoefin.com (now findanadvisor.com)
- **Positioning / headline:** "Find a Financial Advisor."
- **Sub:** "You deserve an advisor you can trust. Find, hire, and invest with vetted, fiduciary financial advisors to help you grow your wealth."
- **Model:** Marketplace/matching, not a direct advisor — useful as a *trust-language* benchmark, not a layout twin.
- **Conversion mechanics:** CTA **"Find an Advisor"** repeated; matching framed on communication style + approach, not just credentials.
- **Trust signals:** Awards (2025 FinTech Breakthrough, ThinkAdvisor Luminaries, NerdWallet "Best Online Financial Advisor" 2022–24), dated 5-star reviews, Vanguard "+3% returns" stat. Heavy SEC/FINRA/SIPC + hypothetical-return disclaimers.
- **Lesson:** "**Fiduciary** advisor **you can trust**" is the dominant trust phrase in the category; dated testimonials + visible disclaimers model compliant social proof.

### 4. Savvy Wealth — savvywealth.com
- **Positioning / headline:** "Experience wealth management, redefined."
- **Sub:** "Savvy combines technology with real-world financial expertise to offer you more freedom and peace of mind."
- **Hero approach:** Tech-forward, dashboard UI screenshots + advisor photography.
- **Color palette:** **Professional navy / dark tones** + whitespace — closest palette to Rae & Co's intended navy.
- **Typography:** Clean modern sans.
- **Conversion mechanics:** CTA **"Find an Advisor."**
- **Trust signals:** **$5B AUM, 100+ advisors**, SEC-registered, **custody with Charles Schwab & Fidelity** (note: same Schwab relationship Rae & Co can cite), SIPC, press (NYSE, Forbes, WSJ).
- **Standout:** Names custodian (Schwab) as a trust signal and leans on a dark/navy palette — directly relevant template for Rae & Co.

### 5. Empower (formerly Personal Capital) — empower.com
- **Headline:** "Whatever your financial happiness looks like, let's get you there." Tagline "Invest well. Live a little.™"
- **CTA:** "Open an account."
- **Trust signals (scale-driven):** "**$2 Trillion** in assets," "helping over **20 million** people," "#1 recommended retirement provider," "93k+ plans." Strong cybersecurity/encryption module.
- **Color/type:** White logo on dark backgrounds, blue accent CTAs, clean sans, diverse lifestyle photography.
- **Lesson:** Massive-scale numbers + a **security/data-protection module** as trust. Rae & Co can't match scale, so it must win on *intimacy and fiduciary clarity* instead.

### 6. Wealthfront — wealthfront.com
- **Positioning:** Tech-first, automated, **flat 0.25%** fee, free financial plan, tax-loss harvesting & direct indexing. Targets hands-off DIY investors who don't want a human.
- **Lesson (contrast):** This is the *anti-positioning* for Rae & Co. Where Wealthfront removes the human, Rae & Co should sell **the human relationship, virtually delivered** — premium advice without the robo coldness.

### 7. Betterment — betterment.com
- **Positioning:** "1M+ people building wealth with confidence and ease." Basic 0.25% / **Premium 0.65%** tier that *adds human advisors*.
- **Lesson:** The "Premium = access to humans" framing validates Rae & Co's entire thesis: human, credentialed advice is the premium upsell. Use that, but make humans the *default*, not the upsell.

### 8. Mercer Advisors — (one of the largest US RIAs)
- **Positioning:** Traditional, comprehensive wealth + estate + tax under one roof; scale and breadth. Not digital-first.
- **Lesson:** Represents the "established big RIA" trust posture (breadth, longevity). Rae & Co should borrow the *comprehensive-under-one-roof* message (investments + retirement income + planning + insurance) without the corporate stiffness.

## 3. Patterns of the Top 10%

1. **Fee transparency as a trust weapon.** The best sites (Facet, Domain Money) *show* the flat-fee model instead of hiding it. "No AUM %," "no commissions," "no hidden fees" badges convert because the category default is opacity. **Do this.**

2. **Quantified value props beat adjectives.** "+3% returns," "2–4x more wealth," "350+ hours saved," "$5B AUM," "24,000 members." Numbers > "personalized, holistic, trusted." Every Rae & Co value prop should carry a figure or a concrete outcome.

3. **One primary CTA, repeated, high-intent and consultative.** "Free Strategy Session" / "Get started" / "Find an Advisor" — never "Contact Us." Personalized/specific CTAs convert dramatically better. Pick ONE primary action and repeat it down the page. Recommended for Rae & Co: **"Book Your Intro Call."**

4. **Layered, compliant social proof in three forms:** (a) named/credentialed expert endorsements, (b) dated client testimonials with disclosure, (c) third-party ratings/press logos. Top sites stack all three near the hero and again before the footer CTA.

5. **Trust module = credentials + custodian + scale + security.** Schwab/Fidelity custody, SEC registration, SIPC, CFP®, AUM/client counts, encryption — presented as a clean typographic "trust lockup," not buried in the footer. Rae & Co's **Charles Schwab** custody + SEC/state registration + CFP credentials should be a dedicated band.

6. **Hero formula:** emotional/aspirational headline (often serif, sometimes italic emphasis on one word) + clear value sub-line + single CTA + immediate proof element. Generous whitespace; lifestyle or refined abstract imagery, never stock-handshake clichés.

7. **Self-qualifying content architecture.** Life-scenario or persona cards ("nearing retirement," "business owner," "recently inherited") let visitors self-select and signal "this firm gets people like me," while guiding to one next step. Authority content (FAQ, planning resources) builds SEO and confidence.

8. **Intentional, restrained motion (premium tier only).** For the luxury aesthetic Rae & Co wants: smooth parallax, subtle hover states, thin gold-line reveals, cinematic section transitions — motion that feels deliberate, never decorative. Pair with Bodoni/Didot-style serif headlines, navy ground, minimal gold accents.

## 4. SEC / RIA Marketing-Rule Compliance Notes (practical, not legal advice)

Under the **SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1)** — actively enforced in 2026 per a Dec 2025 SEC Risk Alert:

- **Testimonials (clients) and endorsements (non-clients) are now allowed**, but each must carry **clear and prominent** disclosures, presented *with* the testimonial — not in fine print, hover text, a separate page, or a footer link.
- Required disclosures at point of display: **(1)** whether the person is a **current client/investor**, **(2)** whether they were **compensated** (cash or non-cash), and **(3)** any **material conflict of interest** (e.g., a referral/solicitor arrangement).
- **Written promoter agreement** required for any compensated promoter, *except* affiliates or **de minimis** compensation (≤ $1,000 in trailing 12 months).
- **Third-party ratings** (e.g., USA TODAY/Statista, NerdWallet, awards) must disclose the date of the rating, who created it, and whether compensation was paid for the rating or its use.
- **Performance / outcome claims** ("+3% returns," "2–4x wealth"): citing third-party studies (Vanguard, etc.) is lower-risk than implying *firm-specific* results. Avoid presenting hypothetical or projected returns without the rule's required context; never cherry-pick. If any firm performance is shown, net-of-fees and prescribed time periods apply.
- **Adviser oversight:** the firm is responsible for ensuring any testimonial/endorsement (including on lead-gen sites, social, "refer-a-friend") complies — substantiation must be available on request.
- **Practical build implication:** design testimonial components with a **built-in disclosure line slot** (e.g., "Current client. Not compensated.") and a ratings component with a date + source slot, so compliance is structural, not bolted on.

> Most common SEC findings in 2026 were simply **missing disclosures at time of display**. Bake the disclosure fields into the CMS components.
